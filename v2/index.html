The motion system

One designed world per film, the camera always moving, every transition carrying meaning. These are the 12 moves the six films are built from.

Used in All

One stage, two lights

A single designed world: a dark, spotlit stage and a warm cream stage that swap on most cuts. Nothing ever cuts to black; every transition is a 2-frame motion-blur streak into a rack focus.

Used in All

Karaoke captions

Every spoken word appears as it is said: the newest word bold, older words fading to grey, one mint pill per sentence on the phrase that matters. Words enter at about 65% scale with a slight skew and settle in 180 ms.

Used in 1, 5, 8

Time Tunnel

The camera flies down a runway of real Radar ads with day markers on the floor. Most ads fold flat and turn grey as they pass, their badges freezing on STOPPED. The winners stay upright and lit. Speed ramps, streaks, depth haze.

Used in 1, 5, 8

Clone Morph

A scan line detects the ad’s zones as wireframes; a headline word flips on its X axis into the new word; the old product bursts into particles that re-form as the new one; everything locks with a magnetic snap and a micro camera shake.

Used in 8

Exploded view

An ad separates into a glossy SURFACE plate and a glowing STRUCTURE plate, with labelled layers on mono leader lines; the surface is swapped while the structure stays.

Used in 8

Timeline transplant

A video ad unrolls into a film strip of shot slots; new shots drop into the same slots and both versions play side by side.

Used in 3, 5

Portal dive

The camera pushes through a laptop or phone bezel into the product UI as a 3D world, and pulls back out, so screen moments never feel like a cutaway.

Used in 3, 10

Casting Wall

A curved 3D wall of creator tiles; phones light up as their creator speaks; at the reveal every tile flies into one formation.

Used in 3, 4, 10

Agent assembly

The agent’s chat becomes physical cards: research chips tick in, casting cards fan out and flip, a voice waveform morphs into script lines, the script rolls onto a film strip, the strip feeds a phone that plays the result.

Used in 4, 10

Match-cut objects

One object carries the story across scenes: the phone showing the creator’s reply turns round to play the finished ad; a tilted bottle becomes the portal to the next creator.

Used in All

Physics stickers and stamps

AI stickers, STOPPED stamps and role labels land with squash, overshoot and a slight tilt, one per beat, never as a pile.

Used in All

Mosaic lockup

Every ad tile used in the film streams into the Ad Studio by Brevo lockup, which collapses into the landing page’s own CTA button.

Rules for every film

  • AI footage keeps a natural, motivated grade. No green glare, tint, glow or LUT on any AI video; brand color lives only in the graphics.
  • Real Ad Studio UI only, rebuilt as HTML replicas from the 2x recordings, with real cursor paths from the click logs. Third-party brands in Radar shown as they are; every price in USD.
  • Narration in a calm, complete, explanatory register (about 2 words a second on camera, about 2.2 for the narrator), continuous from first frame to last.
  • Nothing sits still for more than about 1 second while someone speaks; readable surfaces face the camera within 8 degrees and hold for at least 1.2 seconds.
  • Sound: one quiet cue per meaningful event, music about 27% under the voice and cut on its bar grid, ending on the real ending under the lockup.
